# Capacity options

A **capacity option** is a purchase the farm makes to hold more birds. It
carries four consequences at once: money out now, hours spent building,
birds it lets the farm hold, and what it does to every day afterward. The
four tables below are those four consequences.

The last of them is what separates the two options. One adds a chore round
to the morning and the other takes the round away, so price alone does not
order them.

Costs are James' figures for equipment the farm has either built before or
been quoted for.

The quote covers a pair, and both the price and the assembly hours are
carried here at half so a single coop can be costed on its own. Delivery

The coop runs on a battery and asks for nothing from the pasture. The coop
and the [buried line](/measures/labor) can therefore be argued, funded and
timed one at a time.

## Cost of capacity

| Option | Birds | Capital | Per bird | Daily labor | Per 100 birds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Salatin tractor | 60 | $1,163 | $19 | 33.5 min | 55.8 min |
| Model 600 | 900 | $28,150 | $31 | 30 min | 3.3 min |

Per bird, the two capital costs are the same order. The daily labor is not,
because a tractor needs somebody to carry feed and water to it and a coop
does not.

Both daily figures are measured against the fleet as it stands, which
matters because the first tractor costs more than any that follow it. It
switches on the per-visit cost of running a hose out to pasture, and that
cost is charged once however many tractors come after. Removing the last
tractor therefore returns 1 h 26 rather than the 33.5 min that any other one
returns.

## Effect on the day

| Fleet | Capacity | Day | Blocks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| As it stands | 300 | 5 h 32 | 4 |
| One more tractor | 360 | 6 h 05.5 | 4 |
| Tractors and one coop | 1,200 | 6 h 02 | 4 |
| One coop alone | 900 | 2 h 22 | 4 |
| Two coops alone | 1,800 | 2 h 52 | 4 |

Housing every broiler takes 2 h 40 out of the day and leaves all 4 blocks
standing. Feed and water can be automated away. The eggs still have to be
collected, and somebody still has to shut the coops at dark.

What survives is the laying flock. Of the day that remains, 1 h 37 would be
there with no broilers on the farm at all, brooder included.

## Financing

A tractor is bought outright. A coop is large enough to borrow against, and
the FSA direct Farm Operating rate is 5.25% as of August
2026<sup class="dec d-verified" data-tip="Verified" tabindex="0">+3</sup>.

| Buying | Borrowed | Owner puts in | Monthly | A year | Interest |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| The pair | $56,300 | $0 | $802 | $9,628 | $11,099 |
| The pair | $28,150 | $28,150 | $401 | $4,814 | $5,550 |
| One coop | $28,150 | $0 | $401 | $4,814 | $5,550 |
| One coop | $14,075 | $14,075 | $201 | $2,407 | $2,775 |

The term is 7 years throughout. The owner share is adjustable, so each pair
of rows is the ends of a range rather than two choices.

FSA announces its rates monthly, so the rate above carries the month it was
read. The alternatives are not interchangeable products.

| Alternative | Rate |
|---|---:|
| FSA direct Farm Ownership | 6% |
| FSA Farm Storage Facility, 7 year | 4.375% |
| Bank or credit union, well qualified | 6% to 12% |
| SBA 7(a), variable | 9.75% to 13.25% |

## Still open

- The labor rate is unset. Build hours are counted but never priced, because
  no rate has been agreed for the owners' own time.
- The second coop has never had to win the argument against the capacity the
  2027 plan asks for.
- Eligibility is unresolved. FSA products are not interchangeable, and which
  one a mobile coop qualifies for is a question for the county office rather
  than a figure to assume.
