# Goods

**Goods** are what a bird is sold as. A bird leaves the farm as a whole
roaster, or as two breast packs and a thigh pack and a share of a sausage
batch, and each of those carries its own price and its own weight band.

A band is a weight range with a price attached. Because a heavier pack costs
more, what the farm earns from a bird depends on where its cuts fall across
the bands, and that distribution is the shape of the revenue side.

These are gross figures, and the model applies sell-through to them.

Every figure on this page carries the same rating. The prices are adapted
from another farm's list, and the shares are modeled from a carcass nobody
has weighed, so nothing here has been observed at North Foster Farm.

## Diversions

Not every cut is sold in its most obvious form. A diversion sends a share of
one cut to a different product, and it is where most of the choice in the
catalog lives.

| Diversion | Share |
|---|---:|
| Breasts left bone-in<sup class="dec d-assumed-low" data-tip="Assumed, low confidence" tabindex="0">−2</sup> | 25% |
| Thighs to sausage<sup class="dec d-assumed-low" data-tip="Assumed, low confidence" tabindex="0">−2</sup> | 80% |
| Remaining thighs deboned<sup class="dec d-assumed-low" data-tip="Assumed, low confidence" tabindex="0">−2</sup> | 10% |
| Drumsticks to sausage<sup class="dec d-assumed-low" data-tip="Assumed, low confidence" tabindex="0">−2</sup> | 95% |
| Whole birds spatchcocked<sup class="dec d-assumed-low" data-tip="Assumed, low confidence" tabindex="0">−2</sup> | 30% |

Leaving breasts bone-in keeps the keel and rib meat that otherwise leaves
with the frame. The yield behind the share is an industry standard rather
than a measured one.

The deboning applies to the thighs left after sausage takes its share.

Spatchcocking is one cut with shears.

## Bands

Bands are not a shared axis. Each product has its own ranges and they do not
line up, so a share is always a share of its own product.

## Carcass

The distribution is modeled from a 4.25 lb carcass and a 12 percent
coefficient of
variation<sup class="dec d-assumed-low" data-tip="Assumed, low confidence" tabindex="0">−2</sup>.
Neither figure has been weighed, and the whole band distribution is derived
from them.

The exposure is easiest to see on the boneless breasts. 64.6% of those packs
sit in the 1.00 to 1.24 band at $23, and the band above it pays $4 more. A
carcass heavier than modeled moves packs across that line.

## Prices

Prices are the 2027 list, derived from Pat's per-pound curve at each band
midpoint and rounded to whole dollars.

The list is Pat's, adapted to a different carcass, and it is not yet a
pricing strategy of NFF's own.

Source: Price list and 2027 projection, 23 August 2026.
